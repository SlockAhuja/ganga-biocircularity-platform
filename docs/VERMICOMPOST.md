# BioRiver Vermicomposting & Organic Upcycling Module

This document outlines the scientific basis, mass balance transformations, and conversion parameters of the vermicomposting engine in BioRiver.

---

## 1. Process Overview

Anaerobic digestion of water hyacinth produces two output streams:
1. **Biogas (62% CH₄, 38% CO₂)** $\rightarrow$ Upgraded to purified Bio-CNG (96% CH₄)
2. **Anaerobic Digestate Slurry** $\rightarrow$ Dewatered via screw-press separator into:
   - **Solid Digestate Cake (22-25% Total Solids)**: Primary feedstock for vermicomposting
   - **Liquid Centrate (Fertigation Liquid)**: Rich in mineralized ammonia-N and potassium

---

## 2. Eisenia Fetida Vermicomposting Conversion

The solid digestate cake is co-mixed with cured cow dung slurry (70:30 ratio) and inoculated with *Eisenia fetida* earthworms.

### Mass Balance Parameters:
- **Digestate Yield**: ~85% of fresh hyacinth input
- **Solid Separation Recovery**: 12% dry cake fraction
- **Moisture Loss during Curing**: 45%
- **Compost Conversion Efficiency**: 75%
- **Net Vermicompost Yield**: **23.7 tonnes per 1,000 tonnes fresh hyacinth**

---

## 3. Nutrient Profile & Indian Standards Compliance

BioRiver benchmarks vermicompost output against the **Fertilizer Control Order (FCO 1985)** schedule for organic fertilizers:

| Parameter | BioRiver Output Range | FCO 1985 Standard Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| **Total Nitrogen (N)** | 2.1% - 2.4% (dry wt) | $\ge 1.0\%$ |
| **Total Phosphorus (P₂O₅)** | 1.4% - 1.8% (dry wt) | $\ge 0.5\%$ |
| **Total Potassium (K₂O)** | 1.8% - 2.2% (dry wt) | $\ge 0.5\%$ |
| **C:N Ratio** | 12.5 : 1 | $\le 20.0 : 1$ |
| **Organic Carbon** | 22.4% | $\ge 14.0\%$ |
| **pH** | 6.8 - 7.5 | 6.5 - 7.5 |

---

## 4. Bioactive Vermiwash Extraction

During the vermicomposting cycle, aqueous leachate is collected to produce **Liquid Vermiwash**:
- **Yield**: ~14,000 Liters per 1,000 tonnes fresh biomass
- **Hormonal Profile**: Natural auxins, gibberellins, and cytokinins
- **Application**: 1:10 dilution for foliar spray and disease antagonism
